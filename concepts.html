Core concepts
Business model

From business capability to governed business operation.

The standard keeps the model business-focused and implementation-neutral. A canonical Capability remains the universal business primitive: something meaningful the business can do. Autonomous business also requires common semantics for how consequential capability execution is governed and accounted for.

Normative status: the capability and Business Object concepts below are part of the current Draft 0.2 foundation. Authority, delegation, approval and accountability concepts are the emerging direction for the next normative draft.
ConceptRole in the standard
CapabilityThe universal business primitive: a canonical operation meaningful to the business, independent of how an implementation executes it.
Business DomainAn enduring area of business responsibility and identifier namespace, such as Procurement or Finance.
Business ObjectCanonical business information a capability acts upon, consumes, produces or references.
PartyA reusable business participant such as an organization or person.
RoleThe contextual business role a participant plays, such as Buyer, Supplier, Worker or Approver.
PolicyA business decision or constraint governing whether, when or under what conditions a capability may operate.
AuthorityThe business basis for permitting a party or represented actor to request, approve or perform an operation.
DelegationA business relationship that permits one actor to exercise defined authority on behalf of another within explicit limits.
ApprovalA required business decision that permits an operation to proceed or an effect to occur.
Outcome & EvidenceThe explicit result, resulting business state and business evidence needed for accountability.
ProcessA business orchestration of capabilities and human/system steps toward an outcome; not a separate business primitive.
EventA business-significant occurrence associated with capability execution or Business Object change.

The requester may change. The canonical business meaning and governance requirements should not. The same payment, purchase order, leave request or journal entry must remain governable regardless of how the request originated.

Free-form intent ends at the business boundary

The requester may be non-deterministic. The business contract and governance boundary are not.

An autonomous agent may reason creatively about what should happen next. Before a consequential operation is performed, the business should be able to identify the canonical capability, validate canonical business data, apply authority and policy, check preconditions, obtain required approvals, constrain permitted effects and capture an explicit outcome.

Business governance, not infrastructure governance

ABS standardizes durable business meaning: capabilities, business information, authority, policy, approvals, limits and accountable outcomes. Authentication protocols, IAM products, agent frameworks, policy engines, APIs, databases, middleware and enforcement architecture remain implementation choices.
