Discovery & invocation

The current specification defines the minimum semantics needed to discover, describe and request canonical business capabilities without knowing how the receiving business implements them.

Capability Invocation Envelope

{
  "abcs": "0.2",
  "capability": "procurement.purchase-order.submit",
  "requestId": "01J...",
  "from": { "id": "urn:example:walmart" },
  "to": { "id": "urn:example:walgreens" },
  "payload": { ... }
}

The Draft 0.2 envelope carries capability identity and canonical business payload. from and to, when present, identify business participants rather than network addresses or authentication principals.

From invocation to governed execution

For autonomous business, requesting a valid capability is only the beginning. The next governance layer is intended to determine whether that business operation may proceed under common business semantics for authority, delegation, policy, approval, limits and accountability.

Free-form intent
      ↓
Canonical capability request
      ↓
Business authority + policy + preconditions + approvals + limits
      ↓
Permitted business effect
      ↓
Explicit outcome + evidence
Requester independence: an autonomous agent, workflow, application or person can arrive at the same capability request. Governance belongs to the business operation, not to a particular requester technology.

Optional HTTP binding

Important: HTTP is an example binding, not the business standard. The business semantics remain transport-neutral.
GET  /capabilities
GET  /capabilities/{capability}
POST /capabilities

The same canonical capability and governance semantics could be carried by other transports or invoked internally by a business system without changing their business meaning.

Authentication, security and transport

OAuth, mTLS, JWT, API gateways, messaging, EDI transport, routing, retries, queues, IAM products and middleware are deliberately outside the normative business layer.

Boundary: ABS can standardize business authority, approval and policy semantics. Implementations decide how identities are authenticated, permissions technically enforced, communications secured and evidence persisted.
