Enable autonomous business. Keep business operations governed.

The Autonomous Business Standard (ABS) is being developed as a vendor-neutral foundation for business operations that autonomous agents, workflows, applications and people can request and perform under explicit business governance—so organizations can increase autonomy without surrendering authority, policy, approval or accountability.

Why now

Free-form intent. Governed execution.

Modern autonomous agents can reason, plan and act in ways that are intentionally flexible and non-deterministic. Consequential business operations should cross a stable, governable boundary before they spend money, move inventory, change payroll, approve transactions or affect customers.

Any requester

An autonomous agent, workflow, application, external party or person can request the same canonical business operation.

Governed capability

Business meaning, authority, policy, preconditions, approvals, limits and permitted effects define what may actually happen.

Accountable outcome

The business can distinguish completed, accepted and rejected outcomes and retain the business evidence needed to explain what happened.

The requester may be non-deterministic. The business contract and governance boundary are not.
The emerging model

Meaning. Contract. Governance. Accountability.

Business capability definition is necessary, but it is not sufficient for autonomous business. ABS is evolving toward a complete governed-operation model.

Meaning

What canonical business operation is being requested, expressed with stable vendor-neutral capability identifiers.

Contract

What canonical Business Objects and business information the operation consumes, produces or changes.

Governance

Who may request or authorize it, on whose behalf, under what policy, preconditions, approvals and limits.

Accountability

What outcome occurred, what business state changed, and what evidence is required to explain and review the action.

Draft status

A strong foundation, with governance next.

ABCS Draft 0.2, the current normative specification, establishes canonical capabilities, Business Objects, state, discovery and invocation. The authority, delegation, approval, policy, permitted-effect and accountability model is the active direction for the next normative draft.

Important: ABS is an emerging open standard project. The governance direction described on this site is intentionally visible while it is being designed, but it is not presented as an existing Draft 0.2 conformance requirement.

Business-only boundary

Standardize the business rule, not the enforcement technology.

Would this capability or governance rule still make business sense if humans performed the work manually and all implementation technology disappeared?

If yes, it may belong in ABS. OAuth, JWT, mTLS, API gateways, policy engines, agent frameworks, databases, middleware, workflow runtimes and vendor APIs remain implementation concerns. The standard expresses business authority and control; implementations decide how to enforce them technically.

Business interoperability

Discover. Describe. Govern. Invoke.

The current specification defines Discover, Describe and Invoke semantics. The next governance layer extends that foundation so consequential operations can be evaluated under common business authority, policy and accountability semantics before effects are allowed.

Standard · specification · reference

Open semantics, clear boundaries.

ABS is the emerging open standards project. ABCS is its normative specification. Autonomous Business Reference is a separate non-normative implementation for exploring the model in practice.

Specification

Normative capability, Business Object, schema, state, discovery, invocation and conformance semantics.

Governance

Reference

A deliberately small demonstration of canonical operations, representative guardrails and explicit business outcomes.

Created and open-sourced by Sirvisetti

Contemporary, open and implementation-neutral.

ABS is intentionally ambitious in purpose and precise about maturity. It is not represented as an established industry standard, a standards-body-approved standard or a widely adopted standard. Its goal is to create a common business foundation for the autonomous era without binding adopters to a particular agent, application, protocol or platform.
