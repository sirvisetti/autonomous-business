Governed autonomy
Governed autonomous business · Next-draft direction

Govern the business operation, not the requester.

Autonomous agents can be probabilistic, adaptive and non-deterministic. Business operations that spend money, move inventory, change payroll, approve transactions or affect customers cannot be unconstrained in the same way. ABS is evolving to define the governed business boundary between free-form intent and consequential execution.

Normative status: ABCS Draft 0.2 remains the current normative specification. The broader authority, delegation, approval, policy and accountability model described here is the design direction for the next normative draft and is not yet a Draft 0.2 conformance requirement.
The requester may be non-deterministic. The business contract and governance boundary are not.

Requester-independent governance

The same canonical business operation should be governable regardless of whether it was requested by an autonomous agent, workflow, application, external party or person.

Agent ───────────┐
Workflow ────────┤
Application ─────┼──► Canonical Business Capability ───► Governed Business Outcome
Person ──────────┤
External Party ──┘

Autonomy is a property of how work is requested or performed. Governance belongs to the business operation itself.

What business governance must express

Requester & representation

Who or what requested the operation, which business party is represented, and whether the request is made on behalf of another party.

Authority & delegation

What business authority permits the action, what limits apply, and whether authority has been delegated within a valid business relationship.

Policy & preconditions

The business rules, object state and conditions that must hold before the operation may proceed.

Approval & separation of duties

Whether human or business approval is required and whether initiator, reviewer and approver responsibilities must remain separate.

Permitted effects & limits

What business effects are allowed, including thresholds, scope and constraints that prevent an otherwise valid operation from exceeding authority.

Outcome & evidence

What happened, whether the request was completed or rejected, what business state changed, and what evidence is needed for accountability.

Examples of business-native governance

  • A payment above a defined threshold requires CFO approval.
  • The requester that creates a supplier may not approve that supplier when segregation of duties applies.
  • A worker may submit leave for themselves unless a valid HR role authorizes acting on behalf of another worker.
  • A journal entry must be balanced before it may be posted.
  • An autonomous purchasing agent may submit purchase orders only within delegated buyer authority.

These rules remain meaningful even if every step is performed manually. That is why they belong in a business standard rather than an implementation framework.

What the standard does not govern

ABS does not standardize OAuth, JWT, mTLS, API gateways, IAM products, policy engines, workflow runtimes, agent frameworks, databases, encryption products, message brokers or vendor-specific security models.

Security boundary: ABS expresses business authority, policy, approval, limits and accountability. Implementations decide how those requirements are technically authenticated, authorized, encrypted, enforced and audited.

Business-only scope gate

Would this governance rule still make business sense if humans performed the work manually and all implementation technology disappeared?

If yes, it may belong in the business-governance model. If it exists only because of a protocol, runtime, product or infrastructure component, it remains outside the normative business layer.

From current foundation to governed autonomy

Draft 0.2 establishes the capability, Business Object, state, discovery and invocation foundation. The next normative step is to make consequential capability execution explicitly governable through common business semantics for authority, delegation, policy, approval, permitted effects and evidence.
